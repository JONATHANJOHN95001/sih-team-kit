Team INNOV8 · SIH26042

Team INNOV8 is building the AI-Powered Vernacular Pedagogy and Real-Time Translation Tool for Mother Tongue-Based Primary Education, set by the Government of Jharkhand. A teacher speaks or types a lesson in Hindi, a child reads and hears it in her own language, and answers back. Here is everything the project needs on top of the setup above.

The official problem statement verbatim

SIH 2026 published all 226 problem statements on 21 August. This is ours, copied from the portal. Read it before anything else on this page, because it asks for more than the title suggests.

PS numberSIH26042
TitleAI-Powered Vernacular Pedagogy and Real-Time Translation Tool for Mother Tongue-Based Primary Education
OrganisationGovernment of Jharkhand
DepartmentDepartment of Higher & Technical Education
Category · ThemeSoftware · Smart Education
Dataset or video suppliedNeither. We find our own content, which is why the NCERT work below matters.

The background. Jharkhand’s PALASH programme teaches tribal children in their mother tongue, and it works. What stops it spreading is teachers. Almost everyone posted to a tribal-area primary school was trained in Hindi and does not speak Ho, Mundari or Santhali. So children in more than 5,000 schools are still taught in a language they do not use at home.

What it asks for. Software that lets a teacher who does not speak the language teach in it anyway, with no training first. It has to turn Hindi FLN material, meaning lesson scripts, activity instructions and assessment prompts, into accurate text and spoken audio. It has to carry live two-way conversation in under three seconds. It has to produce bilingual worksheets and flashcards that match NIPUN Bharat. And since these schools have no reliable internet, all of it has to keep working offline on a cheap tablet with 2 GB of RAM running Android 9, once the content has been synced.

What counts as finished. A working app that translates Hindi into at least one tribal language, does live voice translation in under three seconds, prints a bilingual worksheet, and runs fully offline on a low-end Android tablet. Handed in with a demo video and a GitHub repository.

The full list is at sih.gov.in/sih2026PS, 226 statements, 172 software and 54 hardware. Read ours there yourself rather than trusting this summary.
One phrase is worth reading twice: “minimum one tribal language at prototype stage”. Santali on its own is enough to count as finished. Ho and Mundari are named in the background, so they belong in the pitch, but we do not have to ship them.

The statement contradicts itself, and the judges will ask how we handled it. It wants live translation in under three seconds, and it wants the whole thing to work offline. Read the title again we were building the wrong thing

The statement is called AI-Powered Vernacular Pedagogy and Real-Time Translation Tool for Mother Tongue-Based Primary Education. Two of those words are doing more work than we gave them credit for.

Pedagogy means it is a teaching tool, not a translator. Jharkhand is asking for lessons, delivery, comprehension checks and progress, with translation as the layer underneath. A polished translate box answers about half the statement, and half is what the runner-up submits.

Mother Tongue-Based Primary Education is a named policy rather than a description. NEP 2020 requires mother-tongue instruction through Grade 5, which is why a state government is asking for this at all. It sits under NIPUN Bharat, the national mission for Foundational Literacy and Numeracy, whose target is that every child can read with meaning by the end of Grade 3. Naming NEP 2020 and FLN in the pitch shows we read past the title and know which policy this answers.

Our spreadsheet only carries the title. It is scanned text, which is why it reads “Governmcnt” and “Al-Powered”, and the official description is not in it. Get the full text from the SPOC or the portal before we build, because the description normally lists required features.

How many languages we can carry 21, and they are nearly free

We scoped this to Santali, then checked properly. Every one of the 22 scheduled languages was queried against the government catalogue, and 21 of them have the complete pipeline: translate, speak and listen. Only Konkani has nothing.

Supporting all of them costs almost no work. The lesson content is written in Hindi and the output language is a parameter, so a language is a row in an array rather than a feature. We built the hardest one first and the rest came free.

Why the gap exists is worth saying in the pitch. Bhashini covers the Eighth Schedule. Santali was added to it in 2003; the other Jharkhand tribal languages never were, so their absence is constitutional rather than technical. The government knows this, which is why the Ministry of Tribal Affairs built a second platform for exactly those languages. We use both, and our architecture takes a new language the day either one adds it.

How to pitch it. Lead with Jharkhand and demonstrate in Santali, because that is who asked and that is the hard case. Then show the picker. One classroom tool, twenty-one languages, and we solved the lowest-resource one first. Breadth alone reads as shallow; depth in the hardest language with breadth behind it does not.

The eight cards below are superseded read before following them

They describe the stack we planned in August: a React app with an idb cache, CTranslate2, faster-whisper, deployed to Vercel and wrapped for Android. None of that is what got built. The app is native Kotlin with no backend, no hosting and no runtime model, and the reasons are in the corrected stack table further down.

They are kept because the reasoning is still worth reading, and because a few of the warnings turned out to matter more than the plans did: the Ol Chiki font really does have to be bundled or every character renders as an empty box, and NCERT extraction really does drop conjuncts. Do not install anything on the strength of these cards.

1 · Bhashini API key free · required

The Government of India's own language platform does all the translation, speech recognition and speech synthesis. We are not building AI. We are building the teaching tool on top of theirs.

Sign up, verify your email, then go to My Profile and generate a key. You need three values: userID, ulcaApiKey and an Inference API key.

Get a Bhashini key

2 · Noto Sans Ol Chiki easy to miss

Santali is written in the Ol Chiki script, and Windows does not ship a font for it. Without this, every translated word renders as an empty box and the demo dies in front of the judges.

It is free from Google Fonts. Add one line to index.html and set it as the font for any Santali text.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Ol+Chiki&display=swap">

3 · idb offline cache

A small wrapper around IndexedDB for caching lessons and audio so the app works with no connection. localStorage can hold the text, but audio files need IndexedDB.

npm install idb

4 · The same model, offline optional, only if someone is free

IndicTrans2 is the model Bhashini actually serves, and AI4Bharat publish the weights openly, so we could run it ourselves with no key and no network. Take the distilled indic-indic-dist-320M, not the 1B: on a 4 GB card the small one leaves room for Chrome and a dev server, and judging is exactly when everything is open at once. Santali is sat_Olck.

But it is no longer on the critical path. The cache already covers every sentence in the lesson, and a local model only helps for a line nobody has ever translated, while offline. That is a real capability and a poor use of the one week we have. Build it only if someone finishes their ring early.

If it does get built: IndicTransToolkit handles preprocessing and skipping it produces nonsense rather than an error, and CTranslate2 quantises to int8 so it runs on the processor alone.

5 · Speech, in both directions all three confirmed

This was the open question for weeks, and the answer is better than we assumed. Checked against the government's public model catalogue, Bhashini carries all three pieces:

Santali speech output. Bhashini IITM TTS for Santali, FastPitch with a HiFi-GAN vocoder, in male and female voices. This is the one that matters: Santali literacy is low, so a child who cannot yet read Ol Chiki can still hear the lesson.
Santali speech input exists too, in AI4Bharat ASR (conformer) Santhali. We had not counted on this. It means a child can also speak and be understood, which opens up a two-way conversation rather than a one-way translation.
Hindi speech input has five models to choose from, so the teacher speaks instead of typing. faster-whisper stays as the offline fallback, and Vosk at around 50 MB if a laptop struggles.

Because both directions work, the demo can be a genuine spoken exchange: the teacher speaks Hindi, the child hears Santali, the child answers in Santali, the teacher hears Hindi. That is a much stronger thing to put in front of judges than a text box.

6 · Working with no signal the winning answer

Every judge will ask how this works in a village with no connectivity. The strongest possible answer is to turn the wifi off in front of them and keep teaching.

We do this with a cache, not a model in the browser. Every translation and every audio clip is written to IndexedDB as it arrives, and the lesson is pre-loaded before we present. Switch the network off and the lesson still runs, because none of it needs the network a second time.

The earlier plan was Transformers.js, running a quantised model inside the tab. It is the more impressive sentence and it is the wrong call for us: a few hundred megabytes, genuinely awkward to get working, and capable of eating a whole day for a result the cache gets in about an hour. Caching is also simply what a real deployment to rural schools would do, so pre-warming before a demo is honest engineering rather than a trick.

Already written. src/lib/cache.js holds the store and a prewarm() that loads a whole lesson ahead of time. It fails soft, so a blocked IndexedDB costs speed and never function.

7 · Proving it actually works

Translation into Santali will be mediocre, because it is a genuinely low-resource language. Every team hides this. We should measure it instead.

FLORES-200 includes professionally translated Santali sentences. Running our output against it gives a real score, and showing judges a measured number instead of a confident claim is the kind of rigour that separates the top three from everyone else.

Then add a correction box: when a user fixes a bad translation, save it to our glossary and reuse it. That turns the weakness into the feature, and it is about an hour of work.

8 · Where the Python model actually runs easy to get wrong

This is the one piece of the architecture that catches people out. Neither Vercel nor Cloudflare Pages can run IndicTrans2. They host static files and small JavaScript functions; our translation model is PyTorch and needs a real Python process with a couple of gigabytes of memory. Deploying the React app there is correct. Expecting it to carry the model is not.

So the model lives in one of three places, and we should decide which before we start:

On the presenting laptop. Simplest and fastest, no hosting at all, and it makes the offline story honest. This is the default and probably the right answer.
Render if we want it reachable from any phone. Its free tier runs an actual Python service, which is exactly what Vercel cannot do. It sleeps when idle and takes about a minute to wake, so open it before judging starts.
Hugging Face Inference as the no-setup option, at the cost of depending on someone else's uptime during our demo.
Streamlit is worth knowing for hour two. It turns a Python script into a working web page, so we can have a real translate box on screen in about fifteen lines, long before the React app exists. Prove the model works, then build the proper interface around it.

Adi Vaani, the second government platform changes our language story

Bhashini covers the Eighth Schedule. Adi Vaani covers the tribal languages that are not on it, and it is built by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs with an IIT Delhi consortium that includes tribal research institutes in Jharkhand. Launched as a beta in September 2025.

It carries Bhili, Gondi, Santali and Mundari, with Kui and Garo next. Mundari is one of the languages we had written off as unavailable, so this closes one of our five gaps and gives us a route to the rest.

What it can already do. Text translation, OCR, speech to text, speech to speech, video subtitling, a tribal-language dictionary, and an offline mode. Text to speech is marked coming soon, so Santali voice still comes from Bhashini.
Why it is worth more than one extra language. Standing in front of a Jharkhand judge and saying we use Bhashini for the scheduled languages and Adi Vaani for the tribal ones is a much better answer than either alone. It is two pieces of government infrastructure, and it shows we went looking for the tribal-specific one rather than stopping at the obvious platform.

Check it before promising it. It is a beta and we have not tested it. Open adivaani.tribal.gov.in, try a Hindi to Mundari line by hand, and find out whether there is an API or only a web interface. If it is web only, it is a slide rather than a feature.

Somebody has already tested our premise use their number

Learn Your Way is a Google Research experiment that does for textbooks what we are doing for language: it takes raw educational material and regenerates it adapted to the learner, as mind maps, audio lessons and quizzes. It runs on LearnLM, their pedagogy-tuned model family.

They ran a randomised controlled trial against a standard e-reader. Students using it scored 9% higher immediately and retained 11% more after three to five days.

Two reasons this matters to us. It is evidence, from a controlled trial rather than an assertion, that adapting material to the learner improves outcomes, which is the premise our whole project rests on. And it is prior art: know it exists before a judge mentions it, and be ready to say what is different. Ours is mother tongue and offline, for a classroom with one device and a teacher who does not speak the language. Theirs is a personalised reader for someone who already reads the language of instruction.

Look at it before the pitch: labs.google/learn-your-way and the write-up at research.google. Cite the trial, name the source.

What we are actually building decided

A classroom companion. One device at the front of the room. A Hindi-speaking teacher taps or speaks a line of a real NCERT lesson. The class hears it in Santali and sees a picture. A child answers back in Santali and the teacher hears Hindi. Every few lines the device checks whether the class understood. When a translation is wrong the teacher fixes it, and the correction is kept.

Three decisions in that paragraph are worth defending, because each one deletes work:

One device, not one per child. A village primary school has one, not thirty. That assumption also removes accounts, login, per-child sync and profile screens from the entire build.
Audio first, picture second, text third. Children in Class 1 and 2 cannot read in any script. Leading with Ol Chiki looks impressive to a judge and is useless to a seven-year-old, so it is there for the teacher and for older children.
Press to talk, not live captioning. Every Hindi and Santali speech model on the platform is batch rather than streaming, so word-by-word subtitles are not available. Say that plainly in the pitch instead of implying otherwise.

Everything below exists to make ninety seconds work: teacher speaks, class hears Santali, a child answers back, the wifi goes off and it keeps going, a wrong line gets corrected and stays corrected. If a task does not serve that, it does not get built.

Translate the example, not just the words two hours, real differentiator

A Class 2 reader says “A is for Apple”. Translate that literally into Santali and a child in a Jharkhand village gets a correct sentence about a fruit she has probably never seen. The words are right and the teaching has failed.

So the tool also swaps the example. Apple becomes a mango, or a jackfruit, or whatever is actually growing near that school. Snow becomes rain. A city bus becomes a bullock cart. This is what separates a teaching tool from a translator, and it is the clearest possible answer to the word pedagogy in the title.

It is also cheap. A substitution list of forty or fifty terms, applied to the Hindi before it goes to Bhashini. No model, no training. Two hours, and it is the thing a judge will remember.

Where it fits: ring 2, next to the comprehension check. Both are small, both are pedagogy, and together they are the difference between answering half the statement and all of it.

Any chapter, any subject answers the question judges ask

Three hand-entered lessons invites one obvious question: does this only work for the lessons you prepared? Right now the honest answer is yes. Upload changes that. A teacher points it at any chapter of any textbook and it becomes a lesson.

Use OCR, not text extraction, and this is not a preference

When we pulled four NCERT chapters, the PDF text layer dropped conjunct consonants: कयों for क्यों, शबद for शब्द, on roughly half the lines. Those are not Hindi words and Bhashini would translate them into confident nonsense. It is why lessons.json had to be corrected by eye.

But the page renders perfectly. Only the embedded text mapping is broken. OCR reads the rendered pixels, so it gets right what extraction gets wrong. And Bhashini has Hindi OCR, four models covering printed and handwritten, so this stays on the government stack with nothing new to install.

The pipeline. Upload, render the page to an image with PDF.js in the browser, send it to Bhashini OCR, get lines back, the teacher reviews and edits, and it becomes a lesson. The review step is the design rather than a compromise: a teacher should approve what her class is about to hear, and it means OCR does not have to be perfect to be useful.

Primary school has only these four subjects, so all subjects, Class 1 to 5 is a genuinely achievable claim rather than a stretch. Hindi-medium editions exist for maths and EVS too, so the source language stays Hindi throughout.

Maths needs one rule. Never send an equation to a translation model. Mask anything that looks like a numeral or an operator before translating and restore it after, or 2 + 3 = 5 comes back mangled. Same mechanism as the example swap, running in reverse, and about twenty lines. Also decide early whether numbers show as Latin digits, Devanagari, or Ol Chiki's own numerals.

Let the teacher read it too one call, nobody else will do this

Here is a problem hiding in our own demo. The teacher cannot read Ol Chiki. She is holding a device showing Santali she has no way to pronounce, check or correct. She has to trust it completely, which is exactly the position the tool was supposed to get her out of.

The catalogue has transliteration, twenty models from Santali. Show the Santali in Devanagari underneath the Ol Chiki and she can read it aloud herself, hear whether it sounds right, and fix it properly instead of guessing.

One extra API call. It belongs in ring 2 with the other pedagogy work, because it is the difference between a teacher operating a black box and a teacher teaching.

Who does what drag a name to swap

The roles are fixed. The names are not. Drag one name onto another to trade places, or on a phone tap one then tap the other. It saves on this device, so the arrangement is still there tomorrow.

Read the last column before settling. A role that does not match the person is a bottleneck that only shows up at 2am, and five minutes of trading now is worth more than any amount of planning.

Everyone has a second

Pair each role with a backup so nobody is a single point of failure at 3am: Language with Data, Frontend with Design, Lead with Story. The second does not do the work, they only need to know enough to keep it moving while the first is asleep or stuck.

Two rules that outrank who holds which role. Nobody owns two things on the critical path. And Language and Frontend agree the API contract on Monday, in writing, in the repo, because the seam between the Bhashini layer and the screens is where twenty-four hour teams lose the night.

Monday, first hour, all six together: read the official problem statement aloud, trade roles until they fit, write the contract, and say the not-building list out loud. Then Lead runs the Bhashini test while everyone else finishes their setup.

Not building say this out loud on Monday

Login. User accounts. Sync. A lesson authoring tool. A mobile app. Gamification, badges and streaks. Chat. Model training or fine-tuning. Transformers.js. Local IndicTrans2 unless someone is genuinely free. Spline on the landing page.

This list is worth more than the feature list. Every item on it is a plausible-sounding hour that buys nothing in a five-minute demo, and the likeliest way a well-prepared team fails is by adding things in the final week and arriving half-integrated.

Ten rows in this table changed during the build, and the pattern is worth naming. Everything chosen by reasoning about the problem statement survived: offline first, one language done properly, the worksheet, the NIPUN codes, bundling the Ol Chiki font. Everything chosen by reaching for a familiar tool did not: React, Capacitor, Supabase, Vercel, IndexedDB. A web stack wrapped in Capacitor would have made fonts, PDF generation, speech and offline all harder rather than easier. The plan was right about what to build and wrong about what to build it with.
Every row is settled. The open question was whether Santali speech output existed at all. It does, confirmed against the government's public model catalogue, which also lists Santali speech recognition. Nothing in this table is waiting on an unknown any more.

The demo, minute by minute rehearse this

Now that speech works in both directions, the demo stops being a translation box and becomes a conversation. That changes what we show and the order we show it in. Five minutes, rehearsed until nobody has to think.

0:00The problem, in one sentenceA child in a Jharkhand classroom speaks Santali at home and is taught in Hindi. Do not explain the technology yet. Judges have heard forty pitches; open with the child, not the architecture.
0:30Teacher speaksSay a real sentence in Hindi into the microphone. Nobody types anything. The Hindi appears as text while you are still speaking.
0:50Child reads and hearsSantali appears in Ol Chiki, and then plays aloud. Let the audio finish before you speak over it, because the silence is doing the work.
1:20The moment she answers backSpeak Santali into the app and let the teacher hear it in Hindi. This is the whole demo. Most teams show translation going one way; a two-way spoken exchange is a different category of thing, and it is worth pausing for a beat so the room registers what just happened.
2:00Turn the wifi offSwitch off connectivity in front of them, then do the exchange again. Every judge is going to ask how this works where these children live. Answer it before they ask, by doing it.
2:40Fix a wrong wordCorrect a bad translation in the app, then use the phrase again and show the correction stuck. Santali is low-resource and the output will be imperfect, and owning that beats hoping nobody notices.
3:10Show the numberOur FLORES-200 score. One measured figure, honestly stated, lands harder than any adjective.
3:40How it is builtNow the architecture slide: Bhashini for the models, the same models locally for offline, Supabase for the glossary. Thirty seconds. They have already seen it work.
4:20Who it reachesRoughly 7.6 million Santali speakers, and the same pipeline covers the other scheduled languages without new code. Say the number, name the source.
4:45Stop talkingEnd early and leave time for questions. Running to the buzzer reads as poor preparation.
Before you walk up: microphone permission already granted, Supabase awake, the local model already loaded once so the first translation is not the slow one, phone hotspot ready, and the OBS recording open in a background tab. If anything fails live, switch to the recording and keep narrating. Do not debug in front of judges.
